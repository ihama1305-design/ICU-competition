# Presenting Adaptive ICU: a three-minute script

The script follows **Experience the ICU of the future**, the eight-step tour, which runs by itself in about two minutes. Say the lines while each step plays. The tour shows its own captions, so you don't need to read them out.

## Before you start

- **Use Chrome on a laptop.** It's the only browser this site was tested in. Safari, Firefox and phones have not been tested.
- **Open** https://ihama1305-design.github.io/ICU-competition/configurator/?tour=future and let the room load. The tour starts at once. To start it again, press **Restart**, or **Experience the ICU of the future** at the top of the panel.
- **Previous, Pause and Next** are always on screen. If a judge asks something mid-step, press **Pause**.
- No sound plays unless you press a demo-sound button, so the room is quiet while you speak.

## The script

Each step holds for nine seconds once the room has stopped moving, about 15 seconds in all. Each line below is about 30 words, or 12 seconds spoken. If you run long, press **Pause**; the step carries on from where it stopped.

**Before it starts (0:00–0:15)**
> This is Adaptive ICU, our proposal for the fourth-floor ICU at Sheikh Shakhbout Medical City. The room is isolation room W1, built on its measured shape from the hospital's record drawing.

**1. Entering the room (0:15–0:30)**
> The ICU of the future: a lit ceiling tray, a staff screen behind the bed head, a patient wall opposite and a care team's desk. The layout was checked against the space care needs.

**2. Comfort: a scene, the light, the quiet (0:30–0:45)**
> Bringing the outside world into intensive care. The patient picks a forest, and the wall, the ceiling and the light change together, slowly. Drawn scenes, no living plants: a possible benefit, never a guaranteed one.

**3. Asking without speaking (0:45–1:00)**
> Giving every patient a voice. A patient who can't speak asks for water, in English or Arabic, and it reaches the nurse's list. It's simulated, and it doesn't replace the nurse call.

**4. Where the equipment and cables go (1:00–1:15)**
> Less clutter, better access. The pendant keeps the monitor, pumps and outlets off the floor, and each line has a clip, a tag and its slack shown before a move.

**5. The wall and the workstation (1:15–1:30)**
> One wall, more than one purpose. For care it turns to Clinical, with made-up values beside the bedside monitor, whose alarms are unchanged. At the desk, a task lights the cupboard its things are in.

**6. The room's modes, together (1:30–1:45)**
> One choice moves the whole room. One table decides who wins, in five levels with clinical safety first. Comfort steps down, Clinical focuses, Emergency puts access first, and no mode touches a medical alarm.

**7. Before and after (1:45–2:00)**
> Today's room, a typical arrangement, then the new one piece by piece. The difference is not simply how the room looks. It is how the room works.

**8. The closing lines (2:00–2:15)**
> Adaptive ICU: a room that cares, connects and adapts.

**After the tour (2:15–2:45)**
> The ICU of the future should do more than support medical treatment. It should be a place where patients feel heard, healthcare professionals are supported, and technology works quietly in the background. Every patient value you saw is simulated, nothing here is connected to a real device, and a person decides every change.

That leaves 15 seconds spare for a pause or a question.

## If they ask

- **Is any of it real?** No patient data is real, and nothing listens, watches or connects to a device. Each feature's card says whether it's a concept, a simulation or a working tool, and the README's feature table lists them all, including what isn't built.
- **Does it change alarms?** Never. Medical alarms are outside every mode, and the bedside monitor keeps running in every view.
- **Is it AI?** No. Room intelligence is a concept for decision support: its events are buttons, and every notice waits for a person to acknowledge it.
- **The digital twin?** Open it from the panel. It reads the room's hidden state in seven systems, and room health in five separate parts that are never added into a score.
- **PACS?** Open **OPEN NURSE DASHBOARD** in the panel. Simulated vital signs and the patient's preferences lead to a suggestion such as softer light, and a nurse approves it before anything changes. **PLAY THE 77-SECOND DEMO** runs it end to end.
- **APIS?** **Explore APIS** shows an idea for a gentler way to attach tubes, wires and sensors, with each material's job and its limits.
- **The evidence for the lighting?** Mixed, and the card says so: a pilot in redesigned rooms saw less delirium, and a randomised trial of dynamic light alone found no effect.
- **What isn't built?** The ceiling lift, the admission and room readiness board, and a unit board that ranks patients, which we decided against.
