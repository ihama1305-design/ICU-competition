Ventilation concept plan

This is concept work, not SSMC's mechanical design. The record drawings show no grilles, ducts or air data. Everything here is assumed from published guidance, and none of it is a compliance finding. It needs review by a mechanical engineer and infection control before anyone relies on it.

{{TOTAL}} ceiling grilles for the fourth-floor ICU model: {{SUPPLY}} supply, {{RETURN}} return and {{EXHAUST}} exhaust. They're generated by rule from each room's type. The grilles are hidden in the walkthrough, the renders and the configurator for now, so this plan is the only place they show. The 3D model still builds them from the same list, hidden, and checks them against this plan.

The isolation suites

  • Pressure steps inward. The bedroom is more negative than the ante room, and the ante room more negative than the corridor, so air moves from the corridor toward the patient. The toilet is the most negative. The figures shown are Dubai DHA's recommended gradient, used as an assumption.
  • Exhaust near the head of the bed. International guidance puts the exhaust over the patient's head or low down near the head. Here it's on the ceiling, as near the bed head as the pendant allows. A low-level grille is the alternative, and isn't modelled.
  • Clean air in by the door. Supply is at the door end of the room, so air crosses staff before reaching the patient, then leaves.
  • No return air. These rooms only exhaust, and that air should go directly outdoors on its own system. No duct route or fan is designed here.
  • Exhaust exceeds supply. That's what holds the room negative. It needs monitoring and an alarm in a real room, which this model doesn't show.

Sources, and how far they go

  • ASHRAE 170. Air changes and pressures were read from Table 7.1 of the 2008 edition with addenda. The current edition is 170-2025, which is paywalled and wasn't read, so these values may have changed.
  • International Health Facility Guidelines, Part E (August 2026). Repeats 6 air changes for intensive care rooms, and 12 for negative-pressure isolation with 10 in the ante room, and places the exhaust.
  • Dubai Health Authority, Part D (Isolation Rooms). Gives the −10, −5 and −15 Pa gradient and requires an ante room. These are Dubai's rules, not Abu Dhabi's.
  • Abu Dhabi Department of Health. Its guidance governs this hospital, and it wasn't found or read. It has to be checked before any of this could apply.
  • FGI Guidelines. The edition that adopts these figures wasn't read directly.
