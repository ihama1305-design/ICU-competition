Ventilation concept plan

This is concept work, not SSMC's mechanical design. The record drawings show no grilles, ducts or air data. Everything here is assumed from published guidance, and none of it is a compliance finding. It needs review by a mechanical engineer and infection control before anyone relies on it.

The isolation suites

  • Pressure steps inward. The bedroom is more negative than the ante room, and the ante room more negative than the corridor, so air moves from the corridor toward the patient. The toilet is the most negative. The figures shown are Dubai DHA's recommended gradient, used as an assumption.
  • Exhaust near the head of the bed. International guidance puts the exhaust over the patient's head or low down near the head. Here it's on the ceiling, as near the bed head as the pendant allows. A low-level grille is the alternative, and isn't modelled.
  • Clean air in by the door. Supply is at the door end of the room, so air crosses staff before reaching the patient, then leaves.
  • No return air. These rooms only exhaust, and that air should go directly outdoors on its own system. No duct route or fan is designed here.
  • Exhaust exceeds supply. That's what holds the room negative. It needs monitoring and an alarm in a real room, which this model doesn't show.

Clean air: filters and UV-C

The air is cleaned in the air handling plant, out of sight. This layer adds no grille and no object to any room, so nothing on the plan above changes, and none of it needs anyone to open an isolation room's door. Like the grilles, it's a concept: no plant, duct or filter housing is designed or drawn.

  • Filters do the work. UV-C is an extra. International guidance calls it an additional measure and "not a mandatory requirement", and CDC treats it as a supplement to filtration.
  • Upper-room UV-C isn't modelled. The guidance also allows fittings high on the wall of an isolation or critical care room. This concept keeps the lamps in the plant and the duct, so nothing new is on show in the room.
  • No filter grades from ASHRAE. The copy of ASHRAE 170 read here is Table 7.1 and its notes only. It allows HEPA units as a supplement in an isolation room but holds no filter table, so the grades above come from the international guidelines alone.

Sources, and how far they go

  • ASHRAE 170. Air changes and pressures were read from Table 7.1 of the 2008 edition with addenda. The current edition is 170-2025, which is paywalled and wasn't read, so these values may have changed.
  • International Health Facility Guidelines, Part E (August 2026). Repeats 6 air changes for intensive care rooms, and 12 for negative-pressure isolation with 10 in the ante room, and places the exhaust. The filter stages, the HEPA-filtered isolation exhaust and the UV-C rows were read from it too. Its Annexure A-3, which lists filtration by room, isn't in the file and wasn't read.
  • Dubai Health Authority, Part D (Isolation Rooms). Gives the −10, −5 and −15 Pa gradient and requires an ante room. It asks for the isolation exhaust to be HEPA-filtered and given UV. These are Dubai's rules, not Abu Dhabi's.
  • CDC, Guidelines for Environmental Infection Control in Health-Care Facilities (2003). Treats UV as a supplemental control only, and says not to fog disinfectant in patient-care areas. The air and cleaning recommendations were read; the rest of this long report wasn't.
  • Abu Dhabi Department of Health. Its guidance governs this hospital, and it wasn't found or read. It has to be checked before any of this could apply.
  • FGI Guidelines. The edition that adopts these figures wasn't read directly.
