Findings

What the floor plan means for a redesign.

What we noticed in today's floor plan. These describe the current ICU, not our design.

Observation windows are only 40 cm tall

They run from 1.0 m to 1.4 m high, so staff only see a strip across the bed, not the whole room. These heights still need checking.

Standard room fronts are glazed and about 2.2 m wide

Privacy relies on a curtain behind the glass. That same glass could also let daylight into the corridor.

Rooms are 7.2 to 9.0 m deep

The angled outer wall makes each room a slightly different size, from {{AREA_MIN}} to {{AREA_MAX}} m².

One nurse station and {{NOOKS}} observation nooks

Each nook sits between a pair of rooms, with a window into both.

Method

Built from the real drawings, not traced by hand.

  1. Read the CAD layers

    The hospital floor plan still has its CAD layers, so walls, doors and windows were taken directly from it, at the right scale.

  2. Find every opening

    Each door was matched to its gap in the wall automatically. Door heights come from labels on the plan.

  3. Furnish the rooms

    All {{ROOMS}} patient rooms get the same typical ICU equipment. Desks follow the counters on the plan.

  4. Render and export

    Blender makes the realistic images, with sunlight and {{LIGHTS}} ceiling lights. The same model is used for the walkthrough.

Accuracy

What's measured and what's assumed.

The layout is accurate. Heights and finishes that weren't on the plans are our best guess, and they're marked as such.
