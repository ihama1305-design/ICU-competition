Walkthrough

Drop a person on the floor and walk.

It runs in your browser with nothing to install. Drag Place person onto any open spot and your view drops to their eye level, 1.6 m above the floor.

  • W A S D to walk, Shift to run, drag or ← → to look. Phones get on-screen arrows.
  • V shows the person from behind.
  • Walls, counters and the facade stop you. Doors and glass fronts let you through.
  • The plan in the corner shows where you are. Click it to jump.
  • Click anything to name it and see how sure the model is about it.

Design proposal

Spec a room the way you'd spec a car.

A separate page for trying out a redesign of one room: an isolation room or a standard room, with finishes, lighting schemes, smart glass, a patio and three themes, by day and at night. Each choice shows in 3D straight away, and a link saves the exact room.

See the room from the pillow in Patient view, switch it between Comfort, Privacy, Clinical and Emergency-ready modes, label the kit and its keep-clear areas in Clinical view, drag in items like a privacy screen or hand rub, and compare the result with today's room. Every patient reading in it is simulated.

Findings

What the floor plan means for a redesign.

These come from the drawing, and the model makes them easy to see. They describe the floor as built, not a proposed design.

Observation windows are 40 cm slots

The WA31 tag reads sill 1.0 m, head 1.4 m. From the nook you see a strip across the bed, not the whole room. Confirm this against the window schedule before relying on it.

Standard room fronts are glazed and about 2.2 m wide

Privacy depends on a curtain behind the glass. The same glass could also carry daylight through to the corridor.

Rooms run 7.2 to 9.0 m from front to facade

The slanted facade and stepped corridor give the rooms slightly different shapes, from {{AREA_MIN}} to {{AREA_MAX}} m².

One nurse station and {{NOOKS}} observation nooks

Each nook sits between a pair of rooms, with a window into both.

Method

Read from the drawing, not traced by eye.

  1. Read the CAD layers

    Sheet A1104-a is an AutoCAD PDF that still has its layers. Walls, doors, glazing and joinery come straight from them, and the 9 m structural grid sets the scale.

  2. Find every opening

    Each drawn door leaf finds its opening by casting rays to the jambs along the wall line. Door heights come from the H=2410 tags.

  3. Furnish the rooms

    All {{ROOMS}} patient rooms get the same typical ICU kit, placed from the bed head. Nurse station and nook desks follow the drawn counters.

  4. Render and export

    Blender Cycles renders the scene with sun, sky and {{LIGHTS}} ceiling lights. The same scene exports to the walkthrough, and one spec file controls the heights, materials and positions.

Accuracy

What's measured and what's assumed.

The layout is reliable. Height and finish choices that aren't on these sheets are assumptions, and they're marked here and in the walkthrough.

ItemConfidenceBasis
Walls, room shapes, door positionsHighVector CAD geometry, to about 2–3 cm.
Door head height, 2.41 mHighH=2410 tags on the drawing.
Room fronts: 2.2 m glazed openingMediumWidths are drawn. A breakaway glass door is how the drawn leaf reads.
Observation windows, 1.0–1.4 mMediumWA31 tag read with the sheet's legend. Check the window schedule.
Facade: 700 mm upstand, curtain wall, finsMediumTagged upstand and drawn lines. Mullion and fin spacing are approximate.

Finishes and coloursLowFinish codes are on the plan. The colours are assumed.
Furniture and equipmentLowA typical kit, repeated in every room and fitted to the two isolation rooms. Not an inventory of SSMC's equipment.
Bed position and directionLowHead toward the window, foot toward the front. The two isolation rooms have the bed side-on, near the drawn bed circle. An assumption.
